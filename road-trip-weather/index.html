How to find better weather for your next road trip

A local forecast only answers one question: what happens where you already are. Road trips need a different map — one that shows where the weather gets better within driving distance.

Download on the App Store SunNomad · free for iPhone

The problem with “checking the weather”

Most weather apps are built for staying put. You open the forecast for your city, see rain or grey skies, and decide the weekend is ruined.

For a road trip that is the wrong framing. The useful question is not “Will it rain here?” — it is “Where is it better within a few hours’ drive?” Sunshine, cooler air, less wind, dry ground for camping: those conditions often exist nearby, just not on the pin you checked first.

What “better weather” means on the road

Better is not one number. Depending on the trip, it might mean:

  • More sunshine hours for a coastal weekend
  • Warmer days when winter has settled in at home
  • Cooler, drier air when heat sits over the plains
  • Less rain before you commit to a mountain pass or a wild camp

Distance matters too. A perfect forecast 900 km away is not the same as a good one 180 km away. Road-trip weather is always a trade: conditions versus drive time.

How SunNomad answers that

SunNomad is a weather map built for that trade. You set a radius, pick a day (up to +10), and see destinations that match what you are looking for — not a single city forecast, but a field of options.

Map → filters → place details. From “somewhere better” to “is this worth the drive?”

1. Start from where you are

Open the map, set your location, and choose how far you are willing to drive. That radius turns a vague hope (“maybe the coast is nicer”) into a searchable field.

2. Filter for the weather you actually want

Use weather chips for sun, rain, snow, and more — plus landscape filters for mountains, coast, or lakes. Sort warmer or cooler when the season demands it. Badges such as Worth the Drive, Warm & Dry, Sunny Streak, or Indian Summer highlight conditions that are useful for a trip, not just a temperature number.

Example

Grey Friday in Berlin. You set 250 km, filter for more sun, and the map lights up places that look better for Saturday — before you pack the van on a guess.

3. Open a place and decide

Each destination has forecasts, badges, and Stop & Stay hints — where camping looks realistic, what the alternative is, whether freestanding is an option. That is the step weather apps usually skip: connecting the forecast to a place you can actually go.

Example

Moab looks dry and clear for the day you picked. You open Stop & Stay, check overnight options, then open directions in Apple Maps or Google Maps — your preferred maps app is remembered next time.

4. Or let the dice decide

Feeling Lucky is for when you know the mood — sunshine, cooler air, wilderness, get away — but not the pin. Set the radius, roll, and if the first result is wrong, roll again from that destination. Useful when November sits on the calendar and you need an escape more than a spreadsheet.

A simple checklist before you drive

  • How far are you willing to go — really?
  • Which day matters most (arrival, weekend peak, or the day of a hike)?
  • What does “better” mean this time: sun, cooler, drier, less wind?
  • Is there a place worth the drive, not only a nicer forecast?
  • Where can you stay if the day runs long?

SunNomad is built around those questions. Guest mode covers map, filters, and place details; an account is only needed for favourites and sync.
